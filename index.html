Personal Jarvis Blueprint
Blueprint · Personal AI operating system

A chief of staff that knows you, watches your commitments and builds your path to CTO or CEO

What a "Jarvis" for your career really is, what it connects to, what you have to give it, and how to build it in phases without breaking your employer's rules.

What most people build

A chatbot with a long prompt

Forgets you between sessions, only acts when asked, can't see your calendar or inbox, and has no sense of where your career is going.

What you need

A system of agents with memory

A persistent model of you, connectors into your work and career sources, scheduled routines that run without prompting, and an approval gate before anything leaves your name.

What it does

Six jobs, one assistant

Each job is a specialist agent. A single orchestrator decides which one to wake up and what needs your sign-off.

Identity

Knows you

Holds your goals, values, working style, people map and the way you write. Updates itself as you correct it.

Execution

Commitment tracker

Finds the promises you make in email, chat and meetings ("I'll send the deck Friday"), tracks them and nudges you before they slip.

Growth

Promotion coach

Maps your company's level criteria to evidence you've produced, spots gaps early and builds the case before review season.

Market

Career radar

Watches CTO, VP Eng and CEO roles at target companies, scores fit against your profile and drafts a tailored story for the strong ones.

Voice

Ghostwriter

Drafts emails, posts, board updates and replies in your tone, from a style profile built on your own sent writing.

Preparation

Briefing & prep

Morning brief, meeting prep with attendee context, weekly review of wins, risks and what to delegate.

How it feels

A Tuesday with it running

Most of the value comes from routines that run on a schedule or on an event, not from you typing questions.

06:45

Morning brief

Today's meetings with one-line context per attendee, three commitments due this week, one overnight email that needs you, a CTO role that opened at a target company.

09:55

Meeting prep

Five minutes before the steering committee: last decisions, open actions you own, the metric the CFO asked about last time.

11:30

Commitment captured

You told a peer in Teams "I'll share the vendor comparison by Thursday". It becomes a tracked item with a Wednesday reminder and a draft outline.

15:00

Draft for approval

A reply to the board member's question, written in your voice. You edit two lines and approve. The edit teaches the style profile.

Fri 17:00

Weekly career review

Evidence logged against your next-level criteria, the gap that hasn't moved in six weeks (P&L ownership), and one concrete step to close it.

Logical architecture

Six layers and a governance spine

Read top to bottom: how you talk to it, who decides, who does the work, what it remembers, how it reaches systems, and where data comes from.

Memory follows the three-tier pattern most agent memory systems converged on in 2026: core context always loaded, searchable recall, and long-term archive.

Solution architecture

What actually gets deployed

A small private cloud tenant you own, a model provider on business terms, and connectors that act as you through delegated sign-in.

Data flow

Two loops: learn, then act

The learning loop runs continuously in the background. The action loop runs when a schedule, an event or you start it, and its last step feeds the first loop.

What you give it

You don't train a model. You build a dossier.

Fine-tuning is rarely worth it here. A frontier model plus a well-kept profile, your own writing samples and good retrieval gets you further, and you can correct it in a sentence.

Identity · 2 hours

Profile interview

  • Career thesis: CTO or CEO, by when, what kind of company, sector, geography
  • Non-negotiables: family, location, compensation floor, values
  • Strengths and blind spots in your own words and from 360 feedback
  • People map: manager, skip, sponsors, mentors, key peers, board contacts
Voice · 1 hour

Writing corpus

  • 50–100 sent emails across audiences: team, peers, execs, external
  • Posts, memos, strategy docs you wrote yourself
  • Do and don't list: phrases you'd never use, sign-offs, formality by audience
Growth · 1 hour

Promotion evidence

  • Company level framework for your current and next level
  • Last two reviews and calibration feedback
  • Brag document of wins, with metrics, or permission to build one from Jira and mail
  • Review calendar: when nominations and calibration happen
Market · 1 hour

Career targets

  • Master CV and LinkedIn data export
  • Target company list (30–50) and the roles that count as a step up
  • Role scoring rules: stage, size, remit, reporting line, location, pay
  • Search firms and recruiters you already talk to
Permissions you grant: delegated read access to mail, calendar and chat first. Write access (send, create events, post) comes later and only through the approval gate.

Radar

Polls target companies' public job-board feeds daily, filters by title and level, scores fit against your profile and only surfaces roles above your threshold.

Gap plan

Turns a gap into a 90-day action: volunteer to own a budget line, ask to present at the next board, write one public piece a month.

Story bank

Keeps 10–15 leadership stories in STAR form, updated as new wins happen, ready for interviews and promotion packets.

Trust

Autonomy is earned one rung at a time

Every action type starts low and moves up only after it has been right for a few weeks. Anything that speaks in your name stays at rung 4.

1 · Observe

Read and remember

Index mail, calendar, docs. Build the profile. No output except answers.

2 · Suggest

Point things out

"You promised Sara the deck Friday." Daily brief, role alerts.

3 · Draft

Prepare the work

Reply drafts, meeting prep, tailored CV, promotion packet, all left as drafts.

4 · Act with approval

One tap to send

Sends email, accepts meetings, posts updates after you approve each one.

5 · Act alone

Low-risk only

Files mail, sets your own reminders, blocks focus time, updates its own tasks.

Guardrails

Where this goes wrong, and the fix

Employer policy

Corporate data in a personal tool

Copying work mail into your own cloud can breach your employment terms and data-protection law. Ask IT first. The clean pattern is two tenants: work agent inside the company's environment, personal agent outside, sharing only what you move by hand.

Security

Prompt injection from email

Any inbound email can contain instructions aimed at your agent. Treat all fetched content as data, never as commands, and keep send and forward actions behind approval.

Access

Over-broad permissions

Start with read scopes only. Keep tokens in a vault, rotate them, and keep one switch that revokes everything at once.

Reputation

Wrong voice, wrong person

A draft that sounds off to a board member costs more than the time it saved. Keep a hard rule that nothing reaches execs, board or external contacts without your review.

Career

Job search on a work device

Keep the career radar, CV and recruiter threads in the personal tenant and personal accounts only.

Quality

Confident wrong memory

Every stored fact keeps its source link and date. Monthly, review what it believes about you and delete what's stale.

Commitments

  • Meeting prep
  • Drafts in your voice
Weeks 6–8

Career engine

  • Evidence log against level criteria
  • Target company feeds and fit scoring

  • Weekly career review
Weeks 9–12

Act & harden

  • Approval-gated sending
  • Rung-5 automations for low-risk tasks
  • Audit review, evals, cost tracking
  • Decide what to build custom
Your part

What you need to do this week

  1. Ask IT what's allowed.Which AI tools may touch work mail and chat, and whether a sanctioned option like Copilot already exists.
  2. Write your career thesis in one page.CTO or CEO, timeframe, company type, location, non-negotiables.
  3. Gather the documents.Level framework, last two reviews, master CV, LinkedIn data export, a list of 30–50 target companies.
  4. Export 50–100 sent emailsand a few documents you wrote, across different audiences.
  5. Pick option A, B or Cand set up the personal tenant and accounts separately from work.
  6. Turn on the daily brief first.Use it for two weeks before adding anything that can send on your behalf.
